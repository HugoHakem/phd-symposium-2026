Jennifer Roth serves as the Director of the PRISM team within the Cancer Program at the Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard. Over the last 12 years, she has expanded the PRISM platform, a multiplexed therapeutic mapping screening technology to encompass more than 900 cancer cell lines. This powerful technology correlates PRISM viability results with functional and baseline genomic data from the Cancer Dependency Map to identify the mechanism of action of drugs. Her contributions include co-authoring over 50 publications, and data generated by PRISM has been instrumental in supporting Investigational New Drug (IND) filings.

Before working at the Broad Institute, Jennifer spent over a decade in the pharmaceutical and biotech sectors, specialising in the development of both small and large molecules for oncology and immunology disease areas. During her time at Merck, she was part of the team responsible for the first HDAC inhibitor to receive FDA approval. Her academic background includes a B.S. in Biochemistry from the University of New Hampshire, an M.A. in Biology from Brown University, and an M.B.A. in Strategy and Business Analysis from Boston University.
